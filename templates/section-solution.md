# Section Template: Solution

## Purpose
Describe the proposed solution from the customer's perspective. Focus on the experience, not the technology.

## Target Word Count
400-600 words

## Key Questions to Answer

1. **One-sentence description:** What is this product/feature in plain language?
2. **Customer journey:** Step-by-step, what does the customer experience?
3. **Key capabilities:** What are the 3-5 core features?
4. **Differentiation:** How is this different from alternatives?
5. **Magic moment:** When does the customer realize "this is exactly what I needed"?
6. **Customer quote:** A representative quote for the press release.

## Format Guidance

- Lead with the one-liner, then expand into the journey
- Use numbered steps for the customer journey
- Use a table for features (Feature | Description | Customer Benefit)
- Customer quote should feel authentic and specific

## Good vs. Bad Examples

### Bad Example
> "Our solution uses a microservices architecture with event-driven synchronization and a Kubernetes-native operator pattern to reconcile infrastructure state. Features include: API gateway, state store, reconciliation engine, and dashboard."

**Why it's bad:** Written from the builder's perspective, full of jargon, no customer benefit mentioned, no experience described.

### Good Example
> **One-Liner:** CloudSync automatically keeps your infrastructure configurations identical across every cloud provider, so your team can focus on building instead of babysitting configs.
>
> **Customer Journey:**
> 1. Platform engineer connects their AWS, GCP, and Azure accounts (5-minute setup)
> 2. CloudSync scans and maps all existing configurations, showing a unified view
> 3. Engineer defines their desired state once — CloudSync ensures all providers match
> 4. When drift is detected, CloudSync alerts the team and shows exactly what changed
> 5. One-click remediation brings everything back to the desired state
>
> **Magic Moment:** The first time a drift is detected and auto-remediated before anyone on the team even notices — that's when teams realize they've eliminated an entire category of incidents.

**Why it's good:** Customer-first language, clear step-by-step experience, specific magic moment, no unnecessary jargon.

## Customer Quote Template

> "[How the product changed a specific workflow or outcome], said [Full Name], [Title] at [Company]. [Second sentence about broader impact — time saved, incidents prevented, team happiness]."

Example:
> "We went from spending 12 hours a week on config management to spending zero. CloudSync caught a drift that would have caused a production outage, and fixed it before our on-call engineer even got paged," said Sarah Chen, VP Engineering at DataFlow. "My team finally gets to work on what they were hired for — building product."

## Checklist

- [ ] One-sentence description is clear and jargon-free
- [ ] Customer journey has 4-6 specific steps
- [ ] Features are described by customer benefit, not technical detail
- [ ] Differentiation explains WHY this is better, not just different
- [ ] Magic moment is vivid and specific
- [ ] Customer quote feels authentic and mentions specific outcomes
- [ ] Solution clearly maps back to the problem statement
